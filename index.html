01 — Selected work

A product I’m building, and the kind of work I do on a team.

MOZIC

Own product · Early access

A platform for the working side of music: songs, setlists, rehearsals, room bookings and classes in one place, plus a public directory where artists, rehearsal rooms, studios, teachers, services and venues can be found by city.

Musicians tend to keep their material across chat groups, PDFs and notes apps, while rooms and teachers handle bookings and students by message. Finding a rehearsal room or a luthier in a given city usually means asking around. MOZIC is my attempt to give each of those roles a proper tool, and to connect them.

Visit mozic.app

My role
Creator. I define the product and build it.
Status
Public since May 2026, in early access and free to use. Several tools are labelled “available in trial” while they are refined with real use.
Stack (publicly visible)
Next.js web app on Vercel, Google sign-in, Spanish and English interface.
Who it serves
Musicians and bands, rehearsal rooms and studios, teachers, services such as luthiers or backline, and venues.

Product and technical decisions you can see in the product

  • Private by default

    Songs, setlists, rehearsals and materials stay in the user’s own space. Only what someone explicitly publishes appears on their public page or in the directory.

  • Works on stage, without signal

    Live mode, with auto-scroll, transposition and large reading, runs offline. Rehearsal rooms and venues are the worst place to depend on a connection.

  • Start from the material people already have

    Import from PDF, Guitar Pro, MIDI and MusicXML; ChordPro is preserved when editing; songbooks print or export to PDF; songs and setlists can be exported.

  • One platform, distinct roles

    Separate spaces per project or band. Rooms get schedules, online booking, waitlists and fixed weekly slots; teachers get courses, students and fees; venues publish a calendar and receive date proposals.

  • Built to be found

    Bilingual routes and city-level directory pages, for example rehearsal rooms or music lessons in Rosario, CABA and La Plata, published in a sitemap so search engines can index them.

  • Honest about where it stands

    A public roadmap and changelog, features marked as in trial until real use backs them, early-access terms that ask for no card, and optional cookies that stay off unless accepted.

03 — How I work

A few habits I bring to any team.

  • Done includes observable

    If a feature can fail in production, it should leave traces and logs that explain why. I’d rather spend an hour on that than a night guessing.

  • Write it down

    Setup steps, how services fit together, why a trade-off was made. Short documents that live next to the code save the next person a lot of questions, often me included.

  • Small changes, tests that describe behaviour

    Reviewable pull requests, tests that read like a spec of what the system should do, and a CI pipeline worth trusting.

  • Agree on “done” early

    Development, QA and product looking at the same acceptance criteria before the work starts, not after. It’s the cheapest bug fix there is.

Day to day: Elixir, Phoenix, PostgreSQL, Redis, background jobs and queues, REST APIs, OpenTelemetry, ExUnit, Docker, GitHub Actions. For product work: Next.js. Previously: Ruby on Rails, .NET, Angular.
